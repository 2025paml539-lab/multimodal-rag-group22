## 3. Anchor: domain, users, corpus
- **Domain and user:** Indian banking regulation. A bank compliance or customer-support analyst who needs a sourced answer from long documents. Approved by the instructor by email on 21 Nov 2026.
- **Why retrieval and not fine-tuning:** the sources are long and change, and every answer must name its document and page.
- **Corpus (E1, `results/e1_inventory.json`):** 166 documents parsed, 8,307 pages, 8,306 text, 3,288 table and 377 image elements. By type: 129 RBI Master Directions, 12 RBI Annual Report parts, 22 slide decks. 20 of the decks are synthetic (generated by `scripts/make_sample_decks.py`, labelled `sensitivity: synthetic`) and 18 extra RBI documents were added without instructor approval. The approved corpus is the 130 Master Directions; 129 are present because one PDF was not in the lab copy.

## 4. Corpus and preprocessing
- Manifest `corpus/manifest.csv` with SHA-256 per file. Ontology `ontology.yaml` (document type, topic, sensitivity, source, date, modality, page), attached to every chunk (`src/rag/registry.py`).
- Ingest (`scripts/ingest_corpus.py`): 167 files found, 1 failed (D147, a PyMuPDF error), 20,339 chunks (15,093 text, 4,869 table, 377 image), 80.6 s with 5 parallel workers (`results/ingest_summary.json`).
- **Speed problems we found and fixed (all measured in the lab):** a per-cell table text call rescanned the page for every cell (102 of 112 s on a 70-page PDF); libraries started far more threads than the 7 CPUs the lab allows (pinning to 6 CPUs took one PDF from 68.3 s to 4.8 s); OCR took 9 to 15 s per call in the lab and was therefore skipped for most PDFs.
- **OCR coverage:** only the 20 synthetic decks and the first documents have OCR text. Later PDFs were ingested without OCR.

## 5. Multimodal parsing and embeddings
- PyMuPDF for text and tables, python-pptx for slides, RapidOCR for text inside images, `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` on the GPU for embeddings (`src/rag/parsers.py`, `ocr.py`, `embedding.py`).
- Tables are rebuilt cell by cell because the library's own table text dropped spaces and decimal points ("8.6" became "86"). Found in lab session S2, fixed, and the fix was made 15 times faster after profiling.
- **Chunking (E2, 30 questions, final index, `final_30/e2_chunking.csv`):** 200 words with 40 overlap gives 20,339 chunks, recall@5 0.867, MRR 0.666. 60 words with 10 overlap gives 50,002 chunks, recall@5 0.900, MRR 0.593. Smaller chunks find the right page slightly more often, larger chunks rank it higher. Neither is clearly better.
- **Embedding choice (TEAM):** MiniLM is small and fast. No second embedding model was compared.

## 6. Vector store, memory and retrieval
- Index: numpy dense matrix plus BM25, fused with reciprocal rank fusion; saved as `elements.json` and `dense.npy` (`src/rag/index.py`). Metadata filters exist in code (`filters=`) but no experiment used them.
- **Document title context:** each chunk is indexed with its document title. On 60 synthetic deck questions this took table recall from 0.0 to 1.0 and table correctness from 0.05 to 0.95 (`results/synthetic` against `results/synthetic_v2`).
- **Retrieval (E3, 30 questions, `final_30/e3_retrieval.csv`):**

| Setup | Recall@5 | MRR |
|---|---|---|
| BM25 | 0.933 | 0.706 |
| Dense | 0.767 | 0.573 |
| Hybrid | 0.867 | 0.666 |
| Hybrid, text only | 0.900 | 0.661 |

  BM25 is best. Multimodal hybrid is not better than text-only on this set: only 2 of 30 questions are image-based.

- **Memory (E4, `final_30/e4_transcript.md`):** short-term memory rewrites a follow-up into a stand-alone question. Two real two-turn conversations from verified tables: in conversation 2 the follow-up "And for bucket 3?" is answered correctly with memory (18%) and fails without it ("Not found in the documents."). In conversation 1 the answer without memory is also correct, but it cites a different document (D003, p.26), so it is right by luck.

## 9. Evaluation
- **Golden set:** 30 questions: 19 text, 9 table, 2 image; 15 dev and 15 test. Each answer was opened in the PDF page and re-checked against the page text by script (Vishruth's 8 by Vishruth). The 2 image questions are the only truly image-based ones; the D145 and D146 pages chosen for image questions hold their words in the text layer.
- **Answers (E5, `final_30/e5_summary.json`):** recall@5 0.867, MRR 0.666, correct 0.833, faithful 0.867, cited a retrieved source 0.967. By type, correct: text 0.895, table 0.667, image 1.0. Dev 0.933, test 0.733.
- **The answer check:** the first check gave 0.375 on 8 questions, because it marked "three working days" wrong against "3 working days" and the rupee sign against "Rs". We normalised number words and currency marks. That change was written after we saw the failures. The strict results are kept in `results/s3_strict_match/`.
- **Automatic evaluation (E5B):** an LLM judge with a rubric, which never sees the golden answer, compared with the golden check: agreement 0.867, kappa 0.294. It passes 4 answers the golden check marks wrong: B-03, K-09 and V-06 are formatting differences, where the judge was right; S-03 is a real error (75% instead of 80%) that the judge missed. Limits: the judge is the same model that wrote the answer.
- **Hand review of the 5 answers marked wrong:** 3 are formatting mismatches ("15 percent" against "15 per cent", the rupee glyph read as a backtick, "25,000" without "Rs"). So accuracy by hand review is 28 of 30 (0.933), against 0.833 from the automatic check.

## 11. Failure analysis and limitations
1. **Table slides lost their context (found on the synthetic set).** A table slide does not repeat the deck name, so a question naming the deck ranked the title slide and table recall was 0.0. Fix: index each chunk with its document title. Result: recall 1.0.
2. **A table was not retrieved (K-06).** The equity capital-charge table (D117, p.66) was missed and the model answered with an invented formula ("12.5 times the capital charge"). Cause: many RBI documents have similar capital tables and the question did not name the document. Proposed fix: filter by document type or topic from the question, using the ontology (coded, not automated).
3. **A near-duplicate clause from another document (S-03).** The model answered "75%" from a different document; the gold answer is 80%. RBI documents repeat clauses with different numbers. Proposed fix: a re-ranker, or document-aware filters.
4. **Chart labels and values separated by OCR (lab session S2).** OCR returned numbers and axis labels in different orders, so the model picked the wrong value for the third quarter. Fixed in the later runs by the title context for the synthetic decks; not retested on real charts.
5. **D147 fails to parse** (PyMuPDF error), so the booklet with the most images is not in the index.
- **Limits:** OCR covers few documents; only 2 image questions; one model (qwen3:8b); answer consistency 90%; the guardrail test cases were written by us.
